Salute, Then Hold

Quick and steady, in one breath

Real performance uses both kinds of muscle. The quick ones make the spark and the steady ones make the staying power. Today you train them in a single sequence.

The move is simple. You salute quickly, ten times, then lift and hold for eight seconds, then drop. It feels like a tiny drumroll followed by a long, strong chord.

The switch from fast to slow is the skill. Many men notice that the hold feels fuller right after the salutes, as if the muscles have been warmed up and are listening. That sense of readiness is what you are building.

Keep your breath moving the whole time. A soft "ha" on the salutes and slow breathing on the hold work well. If the hold trembles, drop and start the sequence fresh.

Form checkCrisp salutes first, then a smooth hold. Keep your breath moving through both.
