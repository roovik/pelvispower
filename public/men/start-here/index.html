How the Month Works

The month has five Levels of six days each. Days one through five of a Level are training days. The sixth day is Recess.

A Day Looks Like This

Every day has the same simple shape. It starts with a short coaching note about one idea and one tip. Then comes Today's Set, with your position, a garment suggestion, the moves, a Pleasure Dial and one variation.

Warm up with five slow breaths and a Soft Drop. Do the set. Cool down with another Soft Drop. Write one line in the Log, with the numbers.

The Pleasure Dial runs from 1 to 5. At 1 you simply count and squeeze. At 5 you add attention, imagination and any warm thoughts that wander in.

The Three Numbers

Proof is fun, so you collect some. On the first day you take three small tests and write the numbers on the left side of the Before and After page. They are your longest hold in seconds, your salutes in thirty seconds, and how many small steps up you can lift.

At the end of the month you take the same tests and write the new numbers on the right side. The difference is yours to enjoy, and it becomes the transcript on your diploma.

Training Alone, in Pairs Or in a Class

This book works three ways, and each one is a good way.

Alone. The default is you, a comfortable spot and three to ten minutes. It is private, quiet and entirely yours.

In pairs. A friend, a brother, a partner or a training buddy can follow the same Day. You can train in the same room or on a video call, count each other's reps, trade friendly challenges and compare numbers over coffee.

Everyone stays clothed and in their own space, and nobody touches anybody. Your buddy can use this book or PelvisPower for Women.

In a small class. Think of a gym circle. Four to twelve people meet on mats, and a leader reads the day's coaching note and calls the set. The leader counts, keeps the pace and watches the form, and everyone breathes together.

Levels 1 through 3 are made for a class, since every move works sitting, lying or standing. The Edge days in Level 4 and every Recess belong at home, because they are personal. A class simply skips those and returns to the group moves.

A few ground rules keep everyone comfortable. Clothes stay on, voices stay low and anyone can skip any move. Each person's numbers are private unless he chooses to share them.

Keeping You Well

Most men train the floor safely at home. A few signs deserve a visit with a pelvic floor physical therapist or a doctor. Pain in the pelvis or the acorns, blood in your urine or semen, and a lasting change in your erections are the main ones.

A lasting change in erections is worth a check even if you feel fine. It can be an early signal about your heart, your blood pressure or your blood sugar, and catching those early is a gift.

Recovering from prostate or pelvic surgery? Ask your doctor or surgeon about a good starting point. Dealing with long-running pelvic pain? A therapist can tailor this month to you.

Release is part of every session in this book, so strength never turns into tightness. Nothing goes inside, and the toilet stays out of the training. Stop any move that hurts, and rest. A gentle day is a good day.
