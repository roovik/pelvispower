How the Month Works

The month has five Levels of six days each. Days one through five of a Level are training days. The sixth day is Recess.

Level 1 teaches the moves. Level 2 builds endurance. Level 3 adds speed and power. Level 4 trains the muscles while you are aroused.

Level 5 brings it all together and ends with the Final Exam and your diploma.

You learn twelve moves, once, and spend the rest of the month getting better at them. That is the whole secret. A few minutes a day, with small increases, beats one heroic hour every time.

A Day Looks Like This

Every day has the same simple shape. It starts with a short coaching note about one idea and one tip. Then comes Today's Set, with your position, a garment suggestion, the moves, a Pleasure Dial and one variation.

Warm up with five slow breaths and a Soft Drop. Do the set. Cool down with another Soft Drop. Write one line in the Log, with the numbers.

The Pleasure Dial runs from 1 to 5. At 1 you simply count and squeeze. At 5 you add attention, imagination and any warm thoughts that wander in.

The Three Numbers

Proof is fun, so you collect some. On the first day you take three small tests and write the numbers on the left side of the Before and After page. They are your longest hold in seconds, your flicks in thirty seconds, and how many small steps up you can lift.

At the end of the month you take the same tests and write the new numbers on the right side. The difference is yours to enjoy, and it becomes the transcript on your diploma.

Recess

Muscles grow on rest. A floor that only squeezes can get tight, and a tight floor is a grumpy floor. Recess is a day of release, breath and pleasure at your own pace.

Think of the school kind of recess. Nobody gets graded, and everyone is happier afterward.

During Your Period

Your period is no reason to stop and no reason to push. You can pause, power through or find a middle path. It is entirely up to you, and you know your body best.

Many women find that gentle training feels good on period days. Movement can ease cramps, and a Soft Drop is a lovely way to relax a tense belly. Others feel heavy, tender or tired and prefer a rest. Both are fine, and your floor will be waiting.

Here are four easy options. Power through, and do the Day as written. Go gentle, and use the lower end of every count with extra Soft Drops. Swap, and treat the Day as a Recess with slow breathing and a warm hand on your belly. Pause, and pick up where you left off when you are ready.

Pausing costs nothing. Muscles remember, so the month simply stretches a little longer. If you want your three numbers to be fair, take the tests on a day when you feel like yourself.

Wear whatever feels comfortable and secure, with the pad, liner or period pair you usually use. Many women say warmth and pleasure ease cramps, and some feel extra turned on during their period. Others notice no change at all, and that is normal too. Very heavy bleeding or severe pain is worth a chat with your doctor.

Training Alone, in Pairs Or in a Class

This book works three ways, and each one is a good way.

Alone. The default is you, a comfortable spot and three to ten minutes. It is private, quiet and entirely yours.

In pairs. A friend, a sister, a partner or a training buddy can follow the same Day. You can train in the same room or on a video call, count each other's reps, cheer each other on and compare numbers over coffee.

Everyone stays clothed and in their own space, and nobody touches anybody. Your buddy can use this book or PelvisPower for Men.

In a small class. Think of a Pilates studio. Four to twelve people meet on a mat, and a leader reads the day's coaching note and calls the set. The leader counts, keeps the pace and watches the form, and everyone breathes together.

Levels 1 through 3 are made for a class, since every move works sitting, lying or standing. The Edge days in Level 4 and every Recess belong at home, because they are personal. A class simply skips those and returns to the group moves.

A few ground rules keep everyone comfortable. Clothes stay on, voices stay low and anyone can skip any move. Each person's numbers are private unless she chooses to share them.

Keeping You Well

Most women train the floor safely at home. A few signs deserve a visit with a pelvic floor physical therapist or a doctor. Pain with sex, bleeding you cannot explain, or a feeling of pressure or heaviness in your pelvis are the main ones.

Pregnant, or recently postpartum? Ask your doctor or midwife about a good starting point. Recovering from surgery counts too.

Release is part of every session in this book, so strength never turns into tightness. Only outside pressure ever touches your rosebud, and nothing goes inside. Stop any move that hurts, and rest. A gentle day is a good day.
